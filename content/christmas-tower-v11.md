## Prologue: The Tower Speaks

My name is Migdal Eder. In Hebrew it means Tower of the Flock, and I carried that name for thousands of years. Then came one very special night when I was given a new name - *\"The Christmas Tower\"*. That name is a high honor, and it came with important work.

That night was long ago, today I am nothing more than a pile of rocks on a quiet hill, and time has forgotten me altogether.

My foundation was laid so long ago, that it feels as if I was here at the beginning of creation. I was built in the quiet hills of Canaan, south of a town called Salem. Both of those names have changed since then. The hills of Canaan are called Israel now, and Salem is called Jerusalem. Names change here. Hold on to that.

The Master Stonemason who built me chose every stone Himself, working them in patiently, and precise, so that each one would settle against its neighbor and hold firm.

From high above I watched the shepherds guard their flocks from prowling wolves, from wild weather, and from robbers who came only to steal, and destroy.

For many years the shepherds took care of the sheep, their occasional bleating would lull me into a calming and peacefull state, and so I slept.

Wind pushed and my walls started to lean and tumble to the ground. Lightning split the sky and struck me again and again, and fire consumed what was left.

During the lightning flashes I saw strange images. A man hanging on a cross. A King shining in His glory. Sheep scattered across a hillside with no one to gather them. And a voice sounding like a lion roaring over the thunder and rain, calling out:

## Chapter 1: Jacob and Benjamin, Two Names and One Destiny

One day a man pitched his tent in my shadow. His name was Jacob, the grandson of Abraham, and the Master Stonemason had just given him a new name of his own. Israel. He came carrying a grief so heavy I could feel it from where I stood. His wife Rachel had died giving birth to a son.

With her last breath, Rachel named the baby Ben-oni, which means son of my sorrow. Jacob took the boy in his arms and gave him a different name. Benjamin, Son of my right hand.

*Same little boy, two names.*

Israel buried Rachel on the road to Bethlehem and set a stone over her grave so that no one would forget her. There was nothing for me to do but keep watch.

I stood a long while after they had gone. I did not understand yet why the Master Stonemason had set me on this hill, or what He wanted with an old tower made of rock. I only knew that He had put me here with His own hands, and that He was not finished with me.

## Chapter 2: Ruth, a Foreigner Receives a New Family Name

A new morning came up over the hills and laid its light across the barley fields. The heads of grain bowed and lifted in the wind like a field of worshipers. I had watched a hundred harvests come in from where I stood.

This morning brought me something new. A young foreign woman came walking into my fields.

Her name was Ruth, and she had come a long way from home. She was born in Moab, where the people prayed to gods carved out of wood, gods that seem to take more than they give. She married into a family from Bethlehem, my home town. The family had run to Moab to escape the famine in Israel. Then death came for that family, the father and both sons died. Leaving three widows left in the foreign country: Ruth, her sister-in-law, and Naomi, the mother.

Naomi decided to go home, she had left Bethlehem with a husband and two sons, and she was walking back with nothing, and bitterness. When the women of Bethlehem called her by name, she told them to stop. *"Do not call me Naomi"*, she said. *"Call me Mara"*. Naomi means pleasant. Mara means bitter. She had renamed herself out of her own grief.

Ruth could have stayed in Moab. Her sister-in-law did. She could have kept her country, her people, and her gods, and no one would have thought less of her for it. She chose Naomi instead, and she chose Naomi's God.

*Did you notice the name change? Was there one or two name changes?*

So the two of them walked the road back to Bethlehem together. House of Bread, that town is called, and they came into it hungry in every way. I had watched Jacob weep on that same road. Now I watched the two widows walk it.

Ruth went out to the fields every morning after that. She gathered what the harvesters left behind, which was what the poor and the foreigner were allowed to do, and she never once complained about it. I watched her work. There is a kind of faith that works. Hers was the kind that shows up early and stays late.

The field belonged to a man named Boaz. One morning he came out to his harvest, saw her working, and asked whose young woman she was. His workers told him. She was Naomi's daughter-in-law, and she had left her whole country to take care of the old woman who had nothing left.

Boaz walked over and spoke to her kindly.

In time Boaz took Ruth as his wife. A son was born to them, and they named him Obed. Obed became the father of Jesse, and Jesse became the father of David the king.

## Chapter 3: David, Sent to the Fields and Raised to the Throne

The fields were soft and still that morning, and the sun came up warm over Bethlehem. Shepherds moved through the grass the way shepherds always had, counting, calling, keeping the flock together. I had watched their fathers do this, and their fathers before them.

One of them was a boy.

His name was David, the youngest son of a man named Jesse. He sang while he worked. I heard him from where I stood, all those songs going up into the open air with no one to hear them but the sheep and the Master Stonemason he was singing to. His hands were gentle with the lambs and steady when something came out of the hills after them.

His family had given him the sheep to watch. The Master Stonemason had given him something as well, something very special.

---

That morning a prophet named Samuel came down the road to Bethlehem with a horn of oil in his hand. The Master Stonemason had sent him to anoint a king.

Samuel went to the house of Jesse, and Jesse brought out his sons. The oldest came first, tall and strong and exactly what a king is supposed to look like. The Master Stonemason said no. The next one came. No. Seven sons passed before Samuel that day, and He said no to every one of them.

Samuel asked whether these were all of them.

There is one more, Jesse said. The youngest. But he is out with the sheep.

Send for him, Samuel said. We will not sit down until he gets here.

They brought the boy in from my fields, still smelling like the flock, and Samuel poured the oil over his head in front of every brother who had been called ahead of him.

## Chapter 4: The Levitical Shepherds, Lambs Chosen and Set Apart

> **Exodus 12:3, 5 (ESV)**

The years went by, and the throne came, the way I told you it would. And when David was king he remembered me and the fields below, and he gave us to the Levitical shepherds. These holy caretakers would continue a sacred work, tending sheep destined for the Master Stonemason's service in the Temple.

I watched them care for the sheep, and how they carefully handled every lamb the way a father handles a newborn.

Every newborn lamb was inspected, it had to be perfect, without spot or blemish. These lambs were gifts to the Master Stonemason, it was to remember the days that He led them out of Egypt. The best of them were kept for Passover, the feast that remembered His great love and His forgiveness.

They inspected each one over and over, running their hands along its back and down its legs, looking for any flaw. And when they found a lamb that was perfect, they wrapped it gently in swaddling cloths and laid it in a manger inside my walls, keeping it safe and spotless until the appointed day.

I held those lambs through many long nights. Wrapped in cloth. Lying in a manger. Waiting.

I had been watching a long while by then. A boy renamed over his mother's grave. A widow given a family. A shepherd named king. And now lambs, set apart one at a time and laid in my manger to wait. I did not know yet what the Master Stonemason was preparing me to hold.

## Chapter 6: Joseph and Mary, Turned Away and Taken In

Night fell softly over Bethlehem's hills, and I stood in silent watch. Tonight was different: something in me hadn't settled since that man looked up and met my gaze, and some part of me was still standing guard over the fields, the flocks, and all who rested within and outside my walls, waiting to see if what I'd felt was really true.

Mary and Joseph reached Bethlehem at last, and found the town full of travelers. From my hill I could see all of it: the narrow streets packed shoulder to shoulder, families unloading their donkeys, merchants calling out their wares, children weaving through the crowd. Lamps burned in every window, and a lit window meant a full room. I watched one small figure move along the street from door to door, knocking, waiting, knocking again. That was Joseph. Every door he came to was already closed.

Then the two of them turned and left the noise behind them, out past the last lamp and into the cold and the quiet. I had stood on this hill through storm and fire and a thousand ordinary nights. That night I knew, whatever the Master Stonemason had been keeping me for was coming up my road.

So I did the only thing I could do. I let my light spill out of the entrance like a hand held out, and I presented to them an invitation to come, providing strength, warmth, and protection. There is a manger, and there are clean cloths folded beside it. Come in, and let the child be born where the perfect lambs are kept.

---

Every door in town said no. The tower said yes.

The world had no room, The Master Stonemason made room anyway, and He used an old pile of stone on a hill to do it.

## Chapter 7: The Tower Receives Its Name

They came in out of the cold, and I closed around them.

My walls are thick. The wind that was tearing across the hill that night never reached them. There was clean straw on the floor and the smell of it, and the small sounds sheep make when they are settling. I have sheltered a king's flocks and a shepherd's sons. I had never in all my life been so careful.

That night, inside my walls, Mary gave birth to her Son.

They wrapped Him in swaddling cloths. They laid Him in my manger. The same cloths, The same manger, I held a thousand spotless lambs in that stone box and never once understood why, and in that moment I understood everything.

Outside, the fields went quiet. The shepherds out there had no idea yet.

---

Then the baby opened His eyes and looked at me.

Warmth came over me like a blanket laid across my stones, and I knew those eyes. I had seen them in the storm, in the lightning, in the face of the man on the cross and the King shining in His glory.

And I knew the hands. Small and new and curled up inside the cloth, and they were the same hands that chose me out of the rock, and worked me slow, and set every stone of me in place.

The Master Stonemason was lying in my manger.

He built me with those hands. That night He was small enough to be held in mine.

Then He spoke a new name over me, the way He had spoken one over a grieving father's son, and over a widow from Moab, and over a boy with oil still wet in his hair.

*The Christmas Tower.*

## Chapter 8: Good News and Glad Tidings

Inside my walls the child was sleeping. Out in the fields, my shepherds knew nothing about it. They sat with their flocks the way they had on a thousand other nights, the soft bleating of the sheep mingling with the night breeze, and everything was quiet and ordinary and theirs.

Then the sky opened over my fields.

An angel of the Lord stood in the grass, and the brightness of him washed across the flocks and up my stones until every crack in me was lit. The shepherds went down on the ground and covered their eyes and shook. And out of all that burning light came a voice as gentle as a hand on a shoulder.

---

These words came back across the field to me, spoken out loud by a shepherd still on his knees.

*\"swaddling cloths, and a manger!\"*

Then another voice, closer. *\"Could it be, right here in our tower of the flock?\"*

There was one manger a spotless lamb was carried to, wrapped in cloth, and kept until the day it was called for. It was inside me. It had a baby in it now.

Before they could rise, the whole sky filled. Heaven's joy burst over the hills, and a great multitude of angels stood above my fields, praising God and proclaiming:

---

> **Luke 2:14 (ESV)**
>
> *\"Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace among those with whom he is pleased!\"*

The praise of heaven came down on those fields until my old stones rang with it.

And then they were gone, as suddenly as they had come, the night closed in and there was silence. It was only a moment of silence but enough to ponder.

The biggest news in history, and it was given to shepherds instead of kings. The overlooked told first, just like David, watching sheep while his brothers stood in line for the crown. That night, heaven came down into their field and told them before anyone else. I was starting to learn how the Master Stonemason works, He goes to the ones nobody sends for.

## Chapter 9: Worship Christ the King

Then suddenly the silence broke, The shepherds scampered to their feet, and the fear went out of them all at once. The men who had been face down in the grass a moment before were standing and looking at one another with their mouths open.

*\"We must go!\"* one of them said, and his voice was shaking. *\"Let us hurry to Migdal Eder and see this thing the Lord has made known to us.\"*

They came across the fields toward me at a run, and then, close in, they slowed. My old stones were catching the moonlight, and the ground I had stood on for a thousand years was not the ground they had walked out on that evening. They stopped at my doorway and took off their sandals. They had worked in these fields their whole lives, and they came into me that night the way a man comes into a holy place.

---

They stepped inside and found Him, wrapped in swaddling cloths, lying in the manger, exactly as the angel had told them. And these strong men who wrestle lambs went down on their knees in my straw and wept.

I knew what they were seeing.

I had held spotless lambs in that stone box. Every one of them inspected, every one of them perfect, every one of them wrapped in those same cloths and laid in that same manger and called Passover lamb.

Then one of the shepherds began to speak, and the words were older than any of us, older than my foundation, spoken by a prophet who never saw this night and saw it anyway.

The fields were quiet again, and the child slept inside me, and I stood in the dark doing the work I had been built for. I had watched shepherds keep lambs safe on this hill for longer than any of them could remember. That night I finally understood what all of it had been meant for.

## Chapter 10: The Good Shepherd

Many years went by after the night the child was born inside me. I was older and more worn. Cracks ran through my walls where the weather had gotten in, and a few of my stones had worked loose and slid down into the grass. I was still standing, still keeping company with the shepherds and their sheep.

Then one day a man came walking up my road, and He was not alone. Twelve men walked close beside Him, and behind them came a crowd of men and women, hurrying to stay near enough to hear Him.

I watched Him come, and everything in me went still.

I knew that face. I had seen it before in the lightning, in a vision I was given long ago.

And I knew those hands. They were a grown man's hands now, brown and scarred and used to work, but I had held them when they were small enough to close around a finger. I had known them longer than that. He was the Master Stonemason, and He was coming up my road.

A sick man sat off by himself at the edge of the road. He had leprosy, and people with that sickness were made to keep their distance. He was broken down the way I was broken down. Mine was slow years and hard weather. His was a disease that bored into him and ate his skin.

He called out to the Master Stonemason and asked for help. Everyone else stepped back.

The Master Stonemason walked over to him. He reached out His hand, and He touched him, and the sickness went out of the man, and he was made brand new.

## Chapter 11: The Stones Would Cry Out

The seasons turned over me again and again after that day,Then one afternoon He came down my road one last time. He was riding a young donkey, and His face was set toward Jerusalem. A crowd went with Him, and they were singing. They pulled the cloaks off their own backs and threw them down in the dust for the donkey to walk on. I had watched kings' roads my whole life. I had never seen a road laid down like that.

Some men in the crowd told Him to make the singing stop.

> **Luke 19:40 (ESV)**
>
> *\"I tell you, if these were silent, the very stones would cry out.\"*

---

The very stones.

I have been one of those stones since before there was a Bethlehem to look down on. I have stood on this hill through storm and fire and every silent year in between, and I have never once stopped my worship. If every voice in that crowd had gone quiet, I would have sung His arrival out of my own cracked walls and been glad to do it.

And as He passed me, He turned His head and looked at me, the old tower on the hill, He saw me, He saw right through me. He was seeing me as I was in the beginning, strong sturdy, steadfast, brilliant and new. If He would let me, my stones would cry out in praise to Him.

Then He went over the rise, and the road took Him out of my sight, and I could not follow. Towers stay where they are set.

---

I learned the rest, from the people who came back up the road afterward, saying it to each other with their hands over their mouths. They took Him outside the city and they killed Him on a cross, the way I had seen it in the lightning when I was young and understood none of it. The sky went black in the middle of the day. And the ground shook so hard that the rocks in that country split open.

I felt it in my foundation. Every stone of me shuddered, and I knew what it meant, because I had known those hands since the day they set me here.

> **Matthew 27:51 (ESV)**
>
> *The curtain of the temple was torn in two, from top to bottom. And the earth shook, and the rocks were split.*

---

Three days later, He walked out of a grave.

I had called Him the Master Stonemason my whole long life, because that was the only way I knew Him. He chose me out of the rock. He chipped away what did not belong. He set me on this hill with His own hands and told me nothing for a thousand years.

I know the rest of it now.

He is the One who made the heavens and set the stars where they hang. He is the Lamb my manger was built for, and the Good Shepherd who laid down His life for the flock I was raised to watch. He is the King the prophets promised, the Son given, the child born. He is Immanuel, which means God with us. He is the Beginning and the End. He is Jesus, and He saves His people from their sins.

The Master Stonemason has a new name too, and it was His all along.

---

> **Revelation 22:13 (ESV)**
>
> *\"I am the Alpha and the Omega, the first and the last, the beginning and the end.\"*

**Every name in this book was given. His was revealed.**

My stones sleep now. They are scattered on a quiet hill where the grass has grown over them, and no one who passes knows what they are. My work is finished and I am content with how it went. I held the lambs. I held the Lamb. And I was handed a name I did nothing to earn, by the only hands that have ever made anything new.

I am Migdal Eder. I am the Christmas Tower. He called me that, and I carry it still.

## For the Adults

This is a book about names, and every one of them points to Jesus.

Rachel named her dying breath's son Ben-oni, son of my sorrow, and Jacob renamed him Benjamin, son of my right hand. Hold those two names together and you have Christ: the Man of Sorrows who carried our griefs, and the risen Son seated now at the Father's right hand.

Ruth came out of Moab with nothing and was redeemed by Boaz, her kinsman-redeemer, a relative near enough and willing enough to pay a debt he did not owe. Notice that Scripture still calls her Ruth the Moabite after the redemption. She keeps the name she came with, and God writes her into the family line of the King anyway. That is grace.

Samuel poured oil over the head of the youngest son, the one his own father left out in the field, and then that boy went back to the sheep and waited years for a throne. The greater Son of David would come from those same fields.

The Levitical shepherds wrapped spotless lambs in swaddling cloths and laid them in a manger at Migdal Eder to keep them unblemished for the temple. When the angel told the shepherds the sign would be a baby wrapped in cloths and lying in a manger, those men knew exactly which manger to run to. The Lamb of God was laid where the lambs were kept.

A man with leprosy, untouched for years, was touched and made clean and called a son of God.

And an old tower that had done nothing to deserve it was given a new name by the God who built it.

The tower calls Him the Master Stonemason for most of the book, because hands are what a tower can understand. That is deliberate. It holds back the full weight of who He is until the end, where He is named plainly: Jesus, who saves His people from their sins; Immanuel, God with us; the Lamb, the Good Shepherd, the King, the Beginning and the End. The Baby in the manger is God Himself.

**If you are reading this aloud,** the book has two voices. The tower tells the story. A second voice reads the Scripture and talks directly to your children. The children can also play the part of the shepherds and the angels.

**Telling them the gospel.** Three truths, with verses to read together.

**God made us.** He loves us and wants us to know Him.

## A Personal Note from the Author

A few years ago, I attended a chapel service in southwestern Arizona. An older, gentle pastor walked to the stage, placed his Bible on the podium, and never opened it. He began pacing slowly across the stage, quoting Scripture word for word from memory. His voice carried both authority and tenderness. That morning, he told the story of Migdal Eder, the Tower of the Flock, and how God's perfect plan was woven from Genesis to the Gospels, revealing the birth of His Son.

That message stayed with me. It stirred something deep inside: a longing to tell the story in a way that families could see how every Scripture pointed to the One who was born on a holy night.

With gratitude,

— David Haines, Author of *The Christmas Tower*
