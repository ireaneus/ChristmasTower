## Prologue: The Tower Speaks

Hello, my name is Migdal Eder, which means Tower of the Flock in Hebrew. If you went looking for me today, on the hills south of Jerusalem near the town of Bethlehem, all you would find is rocks scattered across the ground. The tower I once was is gone.

But I was real, and this story is true. Jesus is real, you know because the Bible tells you about Him, and you can feel that He is near. In a small way, it is the same with me. You can't see my walls anymore, but the Bible still calls me by name, and it tells of the Child born in Bethlehem and the shepherds who hurried to find Him.

I watched it all happen from my hill. So come and read along with me, and I will tell you how an old tower came to be known as *\"The Christmas Tower\"*.

---

I wasn't always a pile of rocks. Long ago I was a grand tower, standing tall and strong on those same hills. The Master Stonemason built me Himself. He chose every stone and set each one patiently in its place.

Back then the land was called Canaan, and the town to the north of me was called Salem. Both of those names have changed since then, Canaan is called Israel now, and Salem is called Jerusalem. My name would change too, on one very special night, and my new name would be a high honor that came with important work. That night was still a long way off, but names change here. Hold on to that.

From high above I watched the shepherds guard their flocks from prowling wolves, wild weather, and robbers. Year after year they cared for their sheep, and the soft sound of bleating lulled me into a calm and peaceful rest, and so I slept.

## Chapter 1: Jacob and Benjamin, Two Names and One Destiny

On cool nights the shepherds would sit around their fires at the foot of my hill and tell stories. Their favorite stories were about a family the Master Stonemason had chosen as His own.

The family began with a man named Abram. The Master Stonemason promised Abram that his family would grow until there were more of them than stars in the sky. That was a hard promise to believe, because Abram and his wife Sarai were very old and had no children at all. Then the Master Stonemason did something surprising. He gave them both new names. Abram became Abraham, and Sarai became Sarah. Just as He had promised, they had a baby boy in their old age, and they named him Isaac.

When Isaac grew up, he had twin sons, and one of them was named Jacob. Jacob tricked his brother and had to run far from home, but the Master Stonemason never let go of him. One night Jacob wrestled with a man sent from the Master Stonemason, and they wrestled all night long. When morning came, the man gave Jacob a new name too. Jacob became Israel.

As I listened, I began to wonder about the Master Stonemason. He seemed to love giving new names.

## Chapter 2: Ruth, a Foreigner Receives a New Family Name

A new morning came up over the hills and laid its light across the barley fields. The heads of grain bowed and lifted in the wind like a field of worshipers. I had watched a hundred harvests come in from where I stood, but this morning brought something new. Two women were walking up the road toward Bethlehem.

The older woman was Naomi. Years before, during a famine, she had left Bethlehem with her husband and two sons to find food in the land of Moab. Her sons married Moabite women there. Then sorrow came, Naomi's husband died, and both of her sons died after him, until three widows were left alone in a foreign country.

Naomi decided to go home. She had left Bethlehem with a husband and two sons, and she was coming back with nothing. When the women of the town saw her and called her by name, she told them to stop. \"Do not call me Naomi,\" she said. \"Call me Mara.\" Naomi means pleasant, and Mara means bitter. Her grief gave her that new name, and she chose it for herself.

The young woman beside her was Ruth. She was born in Moab, where people prayed to gods carved from wood and stone, gods that took more than they ever gave. Ruth could have stayed there. Her sister-in-law did, Ruth chose Naomi instead, and she chose Naomi's God.

*Did you notice the name change? Who gave Naomi her new name?*

So the two of them walked the road back to Bethlehem together. The name Bethlehem means House of Bread, and they came into it hungry, yet hopeful, trusting God to provide for them.

Ruth went out to the fields every morning to gather the grain the harvesters left behind. The law of Israel allowed the poor and the foreigner to do that. I watched her work, her faith was the kind of faith, that shows up early and stays late.

Of all the fields around Bethlehem, Ruth just happened to pick the one that belonged to a man named Boaz, the same fields I watch over.

One morning Boaz came out to his harvest, saw her working, and asked who she was. His workers told him she was Naomi's daughter-in-law, the one who had left her country to take care of an old woman with nothing left. Boaz walked over and spoke to her kindly.

In Israel, when a man died, a close relative could step in as his family's redeemer. He could buy back the family's land and marry the widow, so that the man's name would live on. Boaz was one of those relatives, and he chose to be Ruth's redeemer. He married her, and they had a son named Obed. Obed became the father of Jesse, and Jesse became the father of David the king.

## Chapter 3: David, Sent to the Fields and Raised to the Throne

The fields were soft and still that morning, and the sun came up warm over Bethlehem. Shepherds moved through the grass the way shepherds always had, counting, calling, and keeping the flock together.

One of the shepherds was just a boy. His name was David, and he was the youngest of the eight sons of a man named Jesse. David's great-grandmother was Ruth, the young woman from Moab who had once gathered grain in these same fields.

David sang while he worked, his songs rising into the open air with no one to hear them but the sheep and the Master Stonemason he was singing to. His hands were gentle with the lambs and strong when a lion or a bear came out of the hills after them.

His family gave him the lowest job there was. The Master Stonemason had a much bigger one in mind.

---

One morning a prophet named Samuel came down the road to Bethlehem with a horn of oil in his hand. The Master Stonemason had sent him to anoint a new king, and He had told Samuel exactly where to look: among the sons of Jesse.

Samuel came to Jesse's house and began to call upon his sons. The oldest son came first, tall and strong, exactly what a king is supposed to look like. Samuel was sure he must be the one, but the Master Stonemason told him to stop looking at the outside. He was looking at the heart.

One by one, seven sons walked past Samuel, and each time the answer was no. Samuel turned to Jesse and asked, \"Are all your sons here?\"

\"There is one more,\" Jesse said. \"The youngest, but he is out keeping the sheep.\"

\"Send for him,\" Samuel said. \"We will not sit down until he comes.\"

They brought the boy in from my fields, still smelling like the flock. The Master Stonemason told Samuel, \"This is the one,\" and Samuel poured the oil over David's head right there in front of all his brothers.

Then David went back to his sheep, with oil in his hair and a promise in his heart.

## Chapter 4: The Levitical Shepherds, Lambs Chosen and Set Apart

The years rolled on like clouds over the hills. The shepherd boy David became king, and his son Solomon built the great Temple in Jerusalem, where the people came to worship the Master Stonemason.

Then a new kind of shepherd came to my fields. They were Levite shepherds, men from the family of Levi, set apart by the Master Stonemason to serve Him. Their flocks were raised for that Temple.

I had watched many shepherds by then, but these were different. They handled every lamb the way a father holds his newborn child.

---

Every new lamb was looked over with great care. The shepherds ran their hands along its back and down each leg, and they checked its eyes, its ears, and its wool, looking for any flaw. A lamb for the Master Stonemason had to be perfect, without spot or blemish.

The very best were saved for Passover. Every spring the people remembered the night long ago when the Master Stonemason brought their families out of Egypt. Each family had marked its door with the blood of a lamb, and the Master Stonemason passed over every house where He saw the blood.

When the shepherds found a perfect lamb, they wrapped it gently in swaddling cloths and laid it in a stone manger inside my walls. There it stayed, safe and spotless, until the appointed day.

## Chapter 5: Mary and Joseph, a Child Named Before He Was Born

It was evening, the sun was sinking behind the hills, and the light spilled across the fields the color of honey. That was when I saw them on the road: a man walking in front, leading a donkey, and a young woman riding it with both hands resting on the child she was carrying.

They had come a long way, all the way from Nazareth, a town in Galilee far to the north. An emperor in faraway Rome had ordered everyone to be counted in their family's hometown, and this man's family came from Bethlehem. He was from the house of David, the shepherd boy who once sang in my fields.

As they came closer, the man looked up and found me on my hill, and he did not look away. His face was heavy with worry, I could feel the weight he was carrying, and I could also tell he was not carrying it alone. He had put his faith and trust in the Master Stonemason.

I looked back at him, steady and strong, the way I had stood for hundreds of years. I wanted him to know there was shelter here, and rest.

He turned back to the road, reached up, and took her hand, and they went on toward the lamps of Bethlehem.

## Chapter 6: Joseph and Mary, Turned Away and Taken In

Awake and watching now, I looked down the hill toward Bethlehem. It was crowded with travelers who had come to be counted. The narrow streets were packed shoulder to shoulder. Families unloaded their donkeys, merchants called out their wares, and children wove through the crowd. Lamps burned in every window, and a lit window meant a full room.

I watched one small figure move from door to door, knocking, waiting, and knocking again. That was Joseph. Every door he came to offered him nothing.

Then the two of them turned and left the noise behind, out past the last lamp and into the cold and the quiet. I had stood on this hill through storm and fire and a thousand ordinary nights. That night I knew that whatever the Master Stonemason had been keeping me for was coming up my road.

So I did the only thing I could do. I let my light spill out of my doorway like a hand held out. Come in, I called without a sound. There is a manger here, with clean cloths folded beside it. Let the child be born where the perfect lambs are kept.

---

Every door in town said no. I said yes.

The world had no room for them, but the Master Stonemason had already made room, and He used an old stone tower on a hill to do it.

## Chapter 7: The Tower Receives Its Name

They came in out of the cold, and I closed around them.

My walls are thick, and the wind tearing across the hill that night never reached them. There was clean straw on the floor, and the small sounds sheep make when they are settling. I had sheltered a king's flocks and a shepherd's sons, but I had never in all my life been so careful.

That night, inside my walls, Mary gave birth to her Son.

They wrapped Him in swaddling cloths and laid Him in my manger. The same cloths. The same manger. I had held a thousand spotless lambs in that stone box and never understood why. In that moment, I understood everything.

Outside, the fields were quiet. The shepherds had no idea yet.

---

Then the baby opened His eyes and looked at me.

Warmth came over me like a blanket laid across my stones, and I knew those eyes. I had seen them in the storm, in the lightning, in the face of the man on the cross and the King shining in His glory.

And I knew those hands. They were small and new and curled inside the cloth, and they were the same hands that chose me out of the rock, shaped me slowly, and set every stone of me in place.

The Master Stonemason was lying in my manger.

## Chapter 8: Good News and Glad Tidings

Inside my walls the child was sleeping. Out in the fields, my shepherds knew nothing about it yet. They sat with their flocks the way they had on a thousand other nights, with the sheep bleating softly in the night breeze.

\"It's cold tonight,\" said one shepherd, pulling his cloak tight.

\"Keep the fire going,\" said another. \"And keep watch.\"

Then the sky opened over my fields.

An angel of the Lord stood in the grass, and his brightness washed across the flocks and up my stones until every crack in me was lit. The shepherds fell to the ground and covered their eyes.

\"What is that?\" cried the youngest shepherd.

\"Get down!\" shouted another.

Then, out of all that light, came a voice, gently speaking.

\"Fear not!\" said the angel.

## Chapter 9: Worship Christ the King

Then suddenly the silence broke, The shepherds scampered to their feet, and the fear went out of them all at once. The men who had been face down in the grass a moment before were standing and looking at one another with their mouths open.

*\"We must go!\"* one of them said, and his voice was shaking. *\"Let us hurry to Migdal Eder and see this thing the Lord has made known to us.\"*

They came across the fields toward me at a run, and then, close in, they slowed. My old stones were catching the moonlight, and the ground I had stood on for a thousand years was not the ground they had walked out on that evening. They stopped at my doorway and took off their sandals. They had worked in these fields their whole lives, and they came into me that night the way a man comes into a holy place.

---

They stepped inside and found Him, wrapped in swaddling cloths, lying in the manger, exactly as the angel had told them. And these strong men who wrestle lambs went down on their knees in my straw and wept.

I knew what they were seeing.

I had held spotless lambs in that stone box. Every one of them inspected, every one of them perfect, every one of them wrapped in those same cloths and laid in that same manger and called Passover lamb.

Then one of the shepherds began to speak, and the words were older than any of us, older than my foundation, spoken by a prophet who never saw this night, but saw it anyway.

## Chapter 10: The Good Shepherd

Many years went by after the night the child was born inside me. I grew older and more worn. Cracks ran through my walls where the weather had gotten in, and a few of my stones had worked loose and slid down into the grass. Still I stood, keeping company with the shepherds and their sheep.

One day a man came walking up my road, and He was not alone. Twelve men walked close beside Him, and behind them came a crowd, hurrying to stay near enough to hear Him.

I watched Him come, and everything in me went still.

I knew that face. I had seen it long ago, in the lightning, in a vision.

And I knew those hands. They were a grown man's hands now, strong and rough from work, but I had held them when they were small enough to curl around a finger. I had known them even longer than that. He was the Master Stonemason, and He was coming up my road.

A sick man sat by himself at the edge of the road. He had leprosy, a sickness of the skin, and people who had it were made to stay far away from everyone else. He was broken down the way I was broken down. Mine came from long years and hard weather. His came from a sickness that would not let go.

\"Please, help me!\" he called out.

Everyone else stepped back.

The Master Stonemason walked straight over to him. He reached out His hand and touched him, and the sickness left the man, and he was made brand new.

## Chapter 11: The Stones Would Cry Out

The seasons turned over me again and again. Then one afternoon He came down my road one last time. He was riding a young donkey, and His face was set toward Jerusalem. A crowd went with Him, singing. They pulled the cloaks off their own backs and spread them in the dust for the donkey to walk on.

\"Blessed is the King!\" the crowd shouted. \"Blessed is the King who comes in the name of the Lord!\"

Some of the religious leaders in the crowd did not like it. \"Teacher,\" one of them said, \"tell your followers to be quiet!\"

> **Luke 19:38-40 (ESV)**
>
> *\"Blessed is the King who comes in the name of the Lord! Peace in heaven and glory in the highest!\" And some of the Pharisees in the crowd said to him, \"Teacher, rebuke your disciples.\" He answered, \"I tell you, if these were silent, the very stones would cry out.\"*

---

The very stones.

I was one of those stones, and I had been since before there was a Bethlehem to look down on. I had stood on this hill through storm and fire and every silent year in between, and I had never stopped worshiping. If every voice in that crowd had gone quiet, I would have sung out of my own cracked walls, and been glad to do it.

As He passed, He turned His head and looked up at me, the old tower on the hill. He saw right past my cracks. He saw me as I was in the beginning: strong, steady, and new.

Then He went over the rise, and the road carried Him out of my sight. I could not follow. Towers stay where they are set.

---

I learned the rest from the people who came back up the road afterward, whispering to each other with their hands over their mouths. They took Him outside the city and put Him to death on a cross, just as I had seen in the lightning when I was young and understood none of it. The sky went dark in the middle of the day.

> **John 19:30 (ESV)**
>
> *When Jesus had received the sour wine, he said, \"It is finished,\" and he bowed his head and gave up his spirit.*

---

Then the ground shook so hard that rocks split open. I felt it in my foundation. Every stone of me shuddered. Cracks ran up my walls, and stones I had held for a thousand years broke loose and tumbled into the grass. In the Temple in Jerusalem, the great curtain tore in two, from top to bottom.

> **Matthew 27:51 (ESV)**
>
> *And behold, the curtain of the temple was torn in two, from top to bottom. And the earth shook, and the rocks were split.*

And I understood. For a thousand years I had held spotless lambs in my manger, one after another, and every one of them had been pointing to Him. Now the true Passover Lamb had given His life, once and for all, for everyone who puts their trust and faith in Him. No lamb would ever need to wait in my manger again.

> **1 Corinthians 5:7 (ESV)**
>
> *For Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed.*

My work was finished, because His was.

## For the Adults

**This is a book about names, and every one of them points to Jesus.** Rachel's Ben-oni, \"son of my sorrow,\" became Benjamin, \"son of my right hand\": the Man of Sorrows now seated at the Father's right hand. Ruth the outsider was redeemed and written into the King's family line. The shepherd boy left out in the field was anointed king. The temple lambs pointed to \"Christ, our Passover lamb,\" and when He said, \"It is finished,\" no more lambs were needed. The tower calls Him the Master Stonemason until the manger, where it knows its Builder. The Baby in the manger is God Himself.

**Could this be the place?** Luke says Jesus was laid in a manger in Bethlehem, the sign the shepherds were told to look for (Luke 2:7-12). Genesis 35:21 and Micah 4:8 name the tower of the flock, Migdal Eder, near Bethlehem. The Mishnah says animals found between Jerusalem and Migdal Eder were set apart for temple sacrifice (Shekalim 7:4). The God who named Bethlehem seven hundred years ahead (Micah 5:2) could surely lay the true Passover Lamb where Passover lambs were kept. The tower's voice is imagined. The places, the people, and the promises are real.

**If you are reading this aloud,** the book has two voices. The tower tells the story. A second voice reads the Scripture and talks directly to your children. Children can read the speaking parts too.

**Telling them the gospel.** Three truths, with verses to read together.

**God made us.** He loves us and wants us to know Him.

---

> **Genesis 1:27 (ESV)**
>
> *So God created man in his own image, in the image of God he created him; male and female he created them.*

> **1 John 3:1 (ESV)**
>
> *See what kind of love the Father has given to us, that we should be called children of God; and so we are.*

**We are created in His image.** See how great a love He has for what He made.

---

> **Romans 3:23 (ESV)**
>
> *For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God.*

> **Romans 6:23 (ESV)**
>
> *For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.*

**We sinned.** We disobeyed our Creator, doing whatever was right in our own eyes.

---

> **Romans 5:8 (ESV)**
>
> *But God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.*

> **1 Corinthians 15:3-4 (ESV)**
>
> *For I delivered to you as of first importance what I also received: that Christ died for our sins in accordance with the Scriptures, that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day in accordance with the Scriptures.*

**Jesus saves us.** He died for our sins, was buried, and rose again, so that we can be forgiven and belong to Him forever.

Then ask them the question the book has been asking the whole way through. Every person in this story was given a new name by God. Would you like Him to give you one?

Pray with them. Then, like the shepherds, go tell somebody.

*David Haines*

## A Personal Note from the Author

A few years ago, I attended a chapel service in southwestern Arizona. An older, gentle pastor walked to the stage, placed his Bible on the podium, and never opened it. He began pacing slowly across the stage, quoting Scripture word for word from memory. His voice carried both authority and tenderness. That morning, he told the story of Migdal Eder, the Tower of the Flock, and how God's perfect plan was woven from Genesis to the Gospels, revealing the birth of His Son.

That message stayed with me. It stirred something deep inside: a longing to tell the story in a way that families could see how every Scripture pointed to the One who was born on a holy night.

With gratitude,

*David Haines, Author of The Christmas Tower*
